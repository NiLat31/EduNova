01

The conjugate-root rule

A quadratic polynomial with real coefficients has one root \(4-3i\). Find its other root, then determine the monic quadratic polynomial.

Worked solution

Step 1: Use conjugates.

For real coefficients, non-real roots occur in conjugate pairs. The other root is \(4+3i\).

Step 2: Form the monic quadratic.

\((x-(4-3i))(x-(4+3i))=((x-4)+3i)((x-4)-3i)=(x-4)^2+9\).

Step 3: Expand.

\(x^2-8x+16+9=x^2-8x+25\).

Other root: \(4+3i\). Polynomial: \(\boxed{x^2-8x+25=0}\).
02

A cubic with one real root

Solve \(x^3-2x^2+5x-10=0\), given that \(x=2\) is a root.

Worked solution

Step 1: Divide by the known factor.

Since \(x=2\), \(x-2\) is a factor. Synthetic division gives quotient \(x^2+5\) and remainder \(0\).

\[\begin{array}{r|rrrr}2&1&-2&5&-10\\&&2&0&10\\\hline&1&0&5&0\end{array}\]

Step 2: Solve the factors.

\((x-2)(x^2+5)=0\). Thus \(x=2\), or \(x^2=-5\), giving \(x=\pm i\sqrt5\).

Roots: \(\boxed{2,\ i\sqrt5,\ -i\sqrt5}\).
03

Build a polynomial from its roots

A monic cubic has real coefficients. Its roots are \(3\) and \(-1+2i\). Find the polynomial in expanded form.

Worked solution

Step 1: Identify the missing root.

The conjugate root is \(-1-2i\). The roots are \(3,-1+2i,-1-2i\).

Step 2: Form the factors.

\(P(x)=(x-3)(x+1-2i)(x+1+2i)\).

\((x+1)^2-(2i)^2=(x+1)^2+4=x^2+2x+5\).

Step 3: Expand.

\((x-3)(x^2+2x+5)=x^3-x^2-x-15\).

Polynomial: \(\boxed{x^3-x^2-x-15=0}\).
04

Factor a cubic using a complex root

Solve \(z^3-2z+4=0\), given that \(1+i\) is a root. Show how the remaining factor is obtained.

Worked solution

Step 1: Add the conjugate root.

Since the coefficients are real, \(1-i\) is also a root.

Step 2: Form the quadratic factor.

\((z-(1+i))(z-(1-i))=(z-1)^2+1=z^2-2z+2\).

Step 3: Divide the cubic.

\(z^3-2z+4=(z^2-2z+2)(z+2)\). Expanding verifies the identity.

Step 4: Solve.

The quadratic gives \(z=1\pm i\), and \(z+2=0\) gives \(z=-2\).

Roots: \(\boxed{1+i,\ 1-i,\ -2}\).
05

A quartic that splits into two quadratics

Find all roots of \(z^4-4z^3+6z^2-4z+5=0\), given that \(2+i\) is a root.

Worked solution

Step 1:

The conjugate root is \(2-i\).

Step 2: Form its quadratic factor.

\((z-(2+i))(z-(2-i))=(z-2)^2+1=z^2-4z+5\).

Step 3: Factor the quartic.

\(z^4-4z^3+6z^2-4z+5=(z^2-4z+5)(z^2+1)\).

Step 4: Solve each quadratic.

\(z^2-4z+5=0\Rightarrow z=2\pm i\). Also \(z^2+1=0\Rightarrow z=\pm i\).

Roots: \(\boxed{2+i,\ 2-i,\ i,\ -i}\).
06

A quartic with a neat factorisation

Solve \(z^4+4=0\). Express the quartic as a product of two quadratic factors before finding the roots.

Worked solution

Step 1: Use difference of squares.

\(z^4+4=(z^2+2)^2-(2z)^2=(z^2-2z+2)(z^2+2z+2)\).

Step 2: Solve the first quadratic.

\(z^2-2z+2=0\Rightarrow z=(2\pm\sqrt{-4})/2=1\pm i\).

Step 3: Solve the second quadratic.

\(z^2+2z+2=0\Rightarrow z=(-2\pm\sqrt{-4})/2=-1\pm i\).

Roots: \(\boxed{1+i,\ 1-i,\ -1+i,\ -1-i}\).
07

Find the remaining root

The polynomial \(P(z)=z^3-5z^2+17z-13\) has real coefficients and \(2+3i\) is a root. Find all its roots.

Worked solution

Step 1:

The conjugate root is \(2-3i\).

Step 2: Multiply the conjugate factors.

\((z-(2+3i))(z-(2-3i))=(z-2)^2+9=z^2-4z+13\).

Step 3: Find the linear factor.

\(z^3-5z^2+17z-13=(z^2-4z+13)(z-1)\).

Step 4: Solve.

\(z-1=0\Rightarrow z=1\).

Roots: \(\boxed{2+3i,\ 2-3i,\ 1}\).
08

Construct and expand a quartic

A monic quartic polynomial with real coefficients has roots \(-2,\ 3,\ 1+i\). Find the fourth root and write the polynomial in expanded form.

Worked solution

Step 1: Find the fourth root.

The conjugate of \(1+i\) is \(1-i\), which must also be a root.

Step 2: Form the polynomial.

\(P(x)=(x+2)(x-3)(x-(1+i))(x-(1-i))\).

\((x-(1+i))(x-(1-i))=(x-1)^2+1=x^2-2x+2\).

Step 3: Multiply the real-root factors.

\((x+2)(x-3)=x^2-x-6\).

Step 4: Expand.

\((x^2-x-6)(x^2-2x+2)=x^4-3x^3-2x^2+10x-12\).

Fourth root: \(1-i\). Polynomial: \(\boxed{x^4-3x^3-2x^2+10x-12=0}\).
