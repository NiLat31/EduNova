CHAPTER 05 • INTEGRATION • TOPIC 01

Trapezium Rule.

Learn how to estimate the area under a curve by dividing the region into equal intervals and replacing sections of the curve with straight lines.

INTRODUCTION

Why do we estimate?

One of the uses of integration is to find the area under a curve. However, in some cases, finding the exact area using integration may not be possible or may be difficult to evaluate directly. In such situations, we can use an estimation method to approximate the area.

One of the methods used to estimate the area under a curve is the Trapezium Rule.

ESTIMATING THE AREA

Dividing the interval

Consider the curve \(y=f(x)\), and suppose that we want to find the area between the curve and the \(x\)-axis from \(x=a\) to \(x=b\).

To estimate this area, we divide the interval from \(a\) to \(b\) into \(n\) equal intervals.

Dividing the interval into equal parts
The straight lines approximate the curve between consecutive points.

Let the width of each interval be \(h\). Since the total width of the interval is \(b-a\), and it has been divided into \(n\) equal parts,

\(h=\frac{b-a}{n}\)

We can therefore mark the \(x\)-coordinates as

\(x_0=a,\quad x_1,\quad x_2,\quad x_3,\quad \ldots,\quad x_{n-1},\quad x_n=b\)

Because the intervals are equal, the distance between every two consecutive \(x\)-coordinates is the same and is equal to \(h\).

CORRESPONDING VALUES

Finding the \(y\)-coordinates

At each of these \(x\)-coordinates, we can find the corresponding point on the curve.

Let the corresponding \(y\)-coordinates be

\(y_0,\quad y_1,\quad y_2,\quad y_3,\quad \ldots,\quad y_{n-1},\quad y_n\)

where

\(y_i=f(x_i)\)

Therefore, each point on the curve can be written in the form \((x_i,y_i)\).

THE APPROXIMATION

Replacing the curve

The curve between two consecutive points is not a straight line. Therefore, finding the exact area of each small section may still be difficult.

Instead, we approximate each small section of the curve by drawing a straight line between two consecutive points.

KEY IDEA

The curved sections are replaced by straight-line segments joining consecutive points on the curve.

For example, consider the two consecutive points

\((x_0,y_0)\quad\text{and}\quad(x_1,y_1)\)

We draw a straight line joining these two points. Together with the \(x\)-axis and the two vertical lines at \(x_0\) and \(x_1\), this forms a trapezium.

The parallel sides of this trapezium have lengths \(y_0\) and \(y_1\), while its width is \(h\).

\(\text{Area of trapezium} =\frac{1}{2}(y_0+y_1)h\)

We can repeat the same process between every pair of consecutive points.

Therefore, the region under the curve is replaced by a series of trapezia.

UNDERSTANDING THE METHOD

Why is it an approximation?

The straight lines do not follow the curve exactly. Therefore, there will usually be a difference between the actual area under the curve and the area of the trapezia.

However, as we increase the number of intervals, each interval becomes narrower. The straight-line approximations then follow the curve more closely, making the estimated area closer to the actual area.

REMEMBER

More intervals generally mean narrower trapezia and a closer approximation to the actual area.

This is the main idea behind the Trapezium Rule: divide the interval into equal parts, replace each section of the curve with a straight line, calculate the area of each resulting trapezium, and add them together.
