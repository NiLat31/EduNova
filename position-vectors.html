Position Vectors.

A point tells us where something is. A position vector turns that location into a vector from the origin, giving us a powerful way to describe movement between two points.

POSITION DISPLACEMENT DISTANCE
ORIGIN → POINT
TOPIC 04

From “where is it?” to “how do I get there?”

Imagine the origin O as your starting reference point. If a point A is somewhere on the coordinate plane, we can draw an arrow from O to A. That arrow is the position vector of A. Once two points have position vectors, finding the displacement from one point to the other becomes a simple subtraction.

THE BIG IDEA

Position tells us where. Displacement tells us how to move.

The position vector starts at the origin. The relative displacement starts at the first point and ends at the second point.

\(\overrightarrow{AB}=\overrightarrow{OB}-\overrightarrow{OA}\)
KEEP THIS IN MIND

Always subtract the starting position from the finishing position.

If you are travelling A → B, think “where I finish” minus “where I started”.

\( \text{displacement}=\text{finish}-\text{start} \)
01

What is a position vector?

Start at the origin. Point to the location.

A position vector is simply the vector that describes the position of a point relative to the origin. If \(A=(5,6)\), then you can reach A from O by moving 5 units in the x-direction and 6 units in the y-direction. Therefore its position vector is \(\overrightarrow{OA}=5\mathbf{i}+6\mathbf{j}\).

Think visually first.

The coordinates are not just two numbers sitting in brackets. They tell you exactly how far the arrow travels horizontally and vertically from the origin.

03

Worked example.

Let the algebra follow the picture.

THE PROBLEM

Two points are given:

\(A=(5,6)\) and \(B=(-19,16)\). Find \(\overrightarrow{AB}\), then find the distance between A and B.

THE JOURNEY A → B
STEP BY STEP
STEP 1 · WRITE THE POSITION VECTORS
\(\overrightarrow{OA}=5\mathbf{i}+6\mathbf{j}\)
\(\overrightarrow{OB}=-19\mathbf{i}+16\mathbf{j}\)
STEP 2 · FINISH − START
\[ \overrightarrow{AB} =\overrightarrow{OB}-\overrightarrow{OA} \]
STEP 3 · SUBTRACT COMPONENTS
\[ (-19-5)\mathbf{i}+(16-6)\mathbf{j} =-24\mathbf{i}+10\mathbf{j} \]
STEP 4 · FIND THE DISTANCE
\[ |\overrightarrow{AB}| =\sqrt{(-24)^2+10^2} =\sqrt{676}=26 \]
FINAL ANSWER \(\overrightarrow{AB}=-24\mathbf{i}+10\mathbf{j}\)
Distance AB = 26 units
Notice what the subtraction is really doing.

The x-component changes from 5 to −19, so the horizontal movement is −24. The y-component changes from 6 to 16, so the vertical movement is +10. We are not memorising a mysterious formula. We are calculating the actual movement needed to travel from A to B.

04

One sentence to remember.

The whole topic can fit inside one idea.

THE EDU NOVA RULE

If the journey is A → B, calculate B − A.

Position vectors tell you where the points are relative to O. Subtracting those positions tells you how to travel from one point to the other.

\(\boxed{\overrightarrow{AB}=\overrightarrow{OB}-\overrightarrow{OA}}\)
