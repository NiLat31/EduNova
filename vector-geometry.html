VECTORS • TOPIC 02

Geometry gives vectors a shape.

Vectors are not only algebraic objects. They can describe the sides, diagonals, midpoints and divisions of geometric figures. In this lesson, we will use a parallelogram as our main geometric model.

01

The parallelogram

Drag the points and watch the vectors update.

Vector addition

From A to C

Instead of drawing \( \mathbf a+\mathbf b \) across the parallelogram, the resultant is shown separately so the geometry stays clean.

\[ \vec{AC} = \vec{AB} + \vec{BC} \]
Key idea: In a parallelogram, moving from \(A\) to \(B\), then from \(B\) to \(C\), is the same overall movement as going directly from \(A\) to \(C\). Therefore \[ \vec{AC}=\vec{AB}+\vec{BC}. \]

04

Midpoint

The midpoint divides a line segment into two equal parts.

Equal division

\(M\) is halfway.

If \(M\) is the midpoint of \(AB\), then the two vectors from \(M\) to the endpoints have equal magnitude and opposite directions.

\[ \vec{AM} = \vec{MB} \]
\[ \vec{OM} = \frac12 \left( \vec{OA} + \vec{OB} \right) \]
\(M\) stays exactly halfway between A and B.
05

Dividing a vector in a ratio

See exactly what the two numbers in a ratio mean.

Ratio division

\(L\) divides \(AB\).

If \(L\) divides \(AB\) in the ratio \(m:n\), then \(AL:LB=m:n\).

2:3 means 2 parts from A to L, then 3 parts from L to B.
\[ \vec{AL} = \frac{m}{m+n} \vec{AB} \]
06

Ratio in vector form

Turn the geometric ratio into a vector equation.

Worked example

\(P\) divides \(AB\) in the ratio \(2:3\)

Let \( \vec{OA}=\mathbf a \) and \( \vec{OB}=\mathbf b \). Find \( \vec{OP} \).

1
Identify the ratio.

\(AP:PB=2:3\), so the whole segment contains \(2+3=5\) equal parts.

\(2+3=5\)
2
Find \( \vec{AP} \).

\(P\) is two of the five parts of the way from \(A\) to \(B\).

\[ \vec{AP} = \frac25\vec{AB} \]
3
Express \( \vec{AB} \).

Since \( \vec{OA}=\mathbf a \) and \( \vec{OB}=\mathbf b \),

\[ \vec{AB} = \mathbf b-\mathbf a \]
4
Find \( \vec{OP} \).

Start at \(O\), go to \(A\), then move from \(A\) to \(P\).

\[ \vec{OP} = \mathbf a + \frac25(\mathbf b-\mathbf a) \]
Therefore: \[ \boxed{ \vec{OP} = \frac35\mathbf a + \frac25\mathbf b } \]
