VECTORS • TOPIC 03

Magnitude of a Vector.

Measure the length of a vector, calculate its direction, and build the unit vector that keeps the same direction with magnitude 1.

Vector directions are measured in degrees, not radians.
03 / Magnitude & Direction

How long is a vector, and where is it pointing?

The magnitude tells us the length of a vector. Its direction tells us the angle it makes with the positive x-axis. Together, these give a complete description of a vector in two dimensions.

01

Magnitude using Pythagoras

The components of a vector form the shorter sides of a right-angled triangle.

Key relationship
\(c^2=a^2+b^2\)

\(|\vec v|=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}\)

The magnitude is the hypotenuse of the right-angled triangle formed by the vector's components.

Example

For \(\vec{AB}=-24\mathbf{i}+10\mathbf{j}\):

1

Use the component values.

\(|x|=24,\quad |y|=10\)
2

Apply Pythagoras.

\(|\vec{AB}|=\sqrt{24^2+10^2}\)
3

Simplify.

\(\sqrt{676}=\boxed{26}\)
02

Distance between two points

The same idea gives the distance formula.

For points \(A(x_1,y_1)\) and \(B(x_2,y_2)\), the horizontal and vertical differences create a right-angled triangle.

\[d^2=(x_1-x_2)^2+(y_1-y_2)^2\]
so
\[d=\sqrt{(x_1-x_2)^2+(y_1-y_2)^2}\]
03

Direction of a vector

In two dimensions, direction is described by an angle measured from the positive x-axis.

One reference angle. Four positions.

First find the acute reference angle using the absolute values of the components.

\[ heta= an^{-1}\left( rac{|y|}{|x|} ight)\]
Quadrant IDirection = \( heta\)
Quadrant IIDirection = \(180^\circ- heta\)
Quadrant IIIDirection = \(180^\circ+ heta\)
Quadrant IVDirection = \(- heta\)
Important: use degrees.

For this vectors topic, direction angles are measured in the degree system. Do not use radians for these direction-angle calculations. A vector with a negative \(j\)-component can be described with a negative angle measured clockwise from the positive \(i\)-axis.

04

Worked direction example

Use the components to locate the vector, then adjust the reference angle according to its quadrant.

For \(\vec{AB}=-24\mathbf{i}+10\mathbf{j}\), the vector lies in Quadrant II.

\[\theta=\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{10}{24}\right)=22.6^\circ\]

Because the vector is in Quadrant II:

\(180^\circ-22.6^\circ=\boxed{157.4^\circ}\)
05

Unit vectors

A unit vector has the same direction as the original vector but a magnitude of exactly 1.

Keep the direction.

Divide a vector by its magnitude to scale its length down to 1.

\[\hat{u}=\frac{\vec v}{|\vec v|}\]

For \(\vec{AB}=-24\mathbf{i}+10\mathbf{j}\), since \(|\vec{AB}|=26\):

\[\hat{u}=\frac{1}{26}(-24\mathbf{i}+10\mathbf{j})\]

Need another magnitude?

Once you have the unit vector, multiply it by the magnitude you want.

For a magnitude of \(182\):

\[182\hat{u}=\frac{182}{26}(-24\mathbf{i}+10\mathbf{j})\]
\[\boxed{-168\mathbf{i}+70\mathbf{j}}\]
