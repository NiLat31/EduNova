FOUNDATION · ALGEBRAIC FORM

Identify the parts.

Given the complex number:

\[z=-7+5i\]

Find:

  1. The real part of \(z\).
  2. The imaginary part of \(z\).
  3. The coordinates of its corresponding point on the complex plane.
Try all three parts in your notebook before revealing the solution.
STEP-BY-STEP SOLUTION
  1. In \(z=a+bi\), the real part is the value of \(a\). Therefore, \(\operatorname{Re}(z)=-7\).
  2. The imaginary part is the coefficient of \(i\). Therefore, \(\operatorname{Im}(z)=5\).
  3. The real part gives the horizontal coordinate and the imaginary part gives the vertical coordinate.
Final answer: \(\operatorname{Re}(z)=-7\), \(\operatorname{Im}(z)=5\), and the point is \((-7,5)\).

FOUNDATION · ALGEBRAIC FORM

Build the complex number.

A complex number has a real part of \(4\) and an imaginary part of \(-9\).

1. Express the complex number in algebraic form.

2. Identify its quadrant on the complex plane.

Remember that the imaginary part is the coefficient of \(i\), including its sign.
STEP-BY-STEP SOLUTION
  1. The real part is \(4\), so the real term is \(4\).
  2. The imaginary part is \(-9\), so the imaginary term is \(-9i\).
  3. Therefore, \(z=4-9i\). Its real part is positive while its imaginary part is negative, placing it in Quadrant IV.
Final answer: \(z=4-9i\), in Quadrant IV.

INTERMEDIATE · POWERS OF \(i\)

Simplify the expression.

Simplify the following expression and write your answer in algebraic form:

\[3i^{17}+2i^{22}-i^{31}\]

For each term, divide the power by \(4\). Use the decimal part of the quotient to identify the remainder, rewrite the exponent, and simplify each term separately.

Use \(i^4=1\). Do not skip the division step. Show how each exponent is rewritten before simplifying.
STEP-BY-STEP SOLUTION · DIVIDE EACH POWER BY 4

We use the fact that \(i^4=1\). Divide each exponent by \(4\). The fractional decimal part multiplied by \(4\) gives the remainder.

Term 1: \(3i^{17}\)

\[17\div4=4.25\]

The decimal part is \(0.25\), and \(0.25\times4=1\). Therefore, the remainder is \(1\):

\[17=4(4)+1\]
\[\begin{aligned}3i^{17}&=3(i^4)^4i\\&=3(1)^4i\\&=3i\end{aligned}\]

Term 2: \(2i^{22}\)

\[22\div4=5.5\]

The decimal part is \(0.5\), and \(0.5\times4=2\). Therefore, the remainder is \(2\):

\[22=4(5)+2\]
\[\begin{aligned}2i^{22}&=2(i^4)^5i^2\\&=2(1)^5(-1)\\&=-2\end{aligned}\]

Term 3: \(-i^{31}\)

\[31\div4=7.75\]

The decimal part is \(0.75\), and \(0.75\times4=3\). Therefore, the remainder is \(3\):

\[31=4(7)+3\]
\[\begin{aligned}-i^{31}&=-(i^4)^7i^3\\&=-(1)^7(-i)\\&=+i\end{aligned}\]

Combine the three simplified terms:

\[3i-2+i=-2+4i\]
Final answer: \(\boxed{-2+4i}\).

04
INTERMEDIATE · MODULUS

Find the distance from the origin.

Given:

\[z=8-6i\]

Find the modulus of \(z\).

The modulus is the distance from the corresponding point to the origin.
STEP-BY-STEP SOLUTION
  1. Identify the real and imaginary parts: \(a=8\), \(b=-6\).
  2. Use the modulus formula: \(|z|=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}\).
  3. Substitute and simplify:
\[\begin{aligned}|z|&=\sqrt{8^2+(-6)^2}\\&=\sqrt{64+36}\\&=\sqrt{100}=10\end{aligned}\]
Final answer: \(\boxed{|z|=10}\).

INTERMEDIATE · MODULUS

Recover the missing component.

A complex number \(z=x+yi\) has a modulus of \(13\). Its real part is \(5\), and its imaginary part is negative.

Find \(z\) in algebraic form.

After finding \(y^2\), remember that the question gives you the sign of the imaginary part.
STEP-BY-STEP SOLUTION
  1. Use \(|z|=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}\), with \(|z|=13\) and \(x=5\).
  2. Substitute:
\[\begin{aligned}13&=\sqrt{5^2+y^2}\\169&=25+y^2\\y^2&=144\\y&=\pm12\end{aligned}\]
  1. The imaginary part is negative, so choose \(y=-12\).
  2. Write the complex number in algebraic form.
Final answer: \(\boxed{z=5-12i}\).

INTERMEDIATE · ARGUMENT

Find the principal argument.

Given:

\[z=1+\sqrt3i\]

Find the principal argument of \(z\) in radians.

First identify the quadrant. Then use the inverse tangent to find the reference angle.
STEP-BY-STEP SOLUTION
  1. The real part is positive and the imaginary part is positive, so \(z\) lies in Quadrant I.
  2. Use the argument relationship:
\[\tan\theta=\frac{y}{x}=\frac{\sqrt3}{1}=\sqrt3\]
  1. Therefore, \(\theta=\tan^{-1}(\sqrt3)=\frac{\pi}{3}\).
  2. Since the point is in Quadrant I, this is already the principal argument.
Final answer: \(\boxed{\operatorname{Arg}(z)=\frac{\pi}{3}}\).

CHALLENGE · QUADRANTS + ARGUMENT

Read the position correctly.

Consider:

\[z=-1-\sqrt3i\]

1. Identify its quadrant.

2. Find its modulus.

3. Find its principal argument in radians.

The principal argument must lie between \(-\pi\) and \(\pi\). Pay close attention to the signs of both components.
STEP-BY-STEP SOLUTION

1. Quadrant

Both the real and imaginary parts are negative. Therefore, \(z\) lies in Quadrant III.

2. Modulus

\[\begin{aligned}|z|&=\sqrt{(-1)^2+(-\sqrt3)^2}\\&=\sqrt{1+3}\\&=2\end{aligned}\]

3. Principal argument

The reference angle is:

\[\alpha=\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\sqrt3}{1}\right)=\frac{\pi}{3}\]

In Quadrant III, the principal argument is negative and measured from the positive real axis:

\[\operatorname{Arg}(z)=-\pi+\frac{\pi}{3}=-\frac{2\pi}{3}\]
Final answer: Quadrant III, \(\boxed{|z|=2}\), and \(\boxed{\operatorname{Arg}(z)=-\frac{2\pi}{3}}\).

FINAL CHALLENGE · COMPREHENSIVE

Everything comes together.

Given the complex number:

\[z=-3+4i\]

Find:

  1. Its real and imaginary parts.
  2. Its coordinates on the complex plane.
  3. Its modulus.
  4. Its quadrant.
  5. Its principal argument in radians.
This final question combines the main ideas from the lesson. Show your full working before revealing the solution.
STEP-BY-STEP SOLUTION

1. Real and imaginary parts

\[\operatorname{Re}(z)=-3,\qquad \operatorname{Im}(z)=4\]

2. Coordinates

The real part gives the horizontal coordinate and the imaginary part gives the vertical coordinate.

\[(-3,4)\]

3. Modulus

\[\begin{aligned}|z|&=\sqrt{(-3)^2+4^2}\\&=\sqrt{9+16}\\&=5\end{aligned}\]

4. Quadrant

The real part is negative and the imaginary part is positive, so the point lies in Quadrant II.

5. Principal argument

First find the reference angle:

\[\alpha=\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{4}{3}\right)\]

In Quadrant II, subtract the reference angle from \(\pi\):

\[\operatorname{Arg}(z)=\pi-\tan^{-1}\left(\frac43\right)\]
Final answer: \(\operatorname{Re}(z)=-3\), \(\operatorname{Im}(z)=4\), coordinates \((-3,4)\), \(|z|=5\), Quadrant II, and \(\boxed{\operatorname{Arg}(z)=\pi-\tan^{-1}\left(\frac43\right)}\).
