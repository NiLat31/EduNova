QUESTION 01 Foundation

Joining two vectors

\(A\), \(B\) and \(C\) are three points. Given that

\[ \vec{AB}=\mathbf a, \qquad \vec{BC}=\mathbf b, \]

find \(\vec{AC}\) in terms of \(\mathbf a\) and \(\mathbf b\).

Solution

To travel from \(A\) to \(C\), travel from \(A\) to \(B\), then from \(B\) to \(C\).

\[ \vec{AC} = \vec{AB}+\vec{BC} \]

Substitute the given vectors:

\[ \vec{AC} = \mathbf a+\mathbf b \]
\[ \boxed{\vec{AC}=\mathbf a+\mathbf b} \]
QUESTION 02 Foundation

Reversing direction

\(A\), \(B\) and \(C\) are points such that

\[ \vec{AB}=\mathbf a, \qquad \vec{AC}=\mathbf b. \]

Find \(\vec{BC}\) in terms of \(\mathbf a\) and \(\mathbf b\).

Solution

Travel from \(B\) to \(A\), then from \(A\) to \(C\):

\[ \vec{BC} = \vec{BA}+\vec{AC} \]

Since \[ \vec{BA}=-\vec{AB}=-\mathbf a, \] we have

\[ \vec{BC} = -\mathbf a+\mathbf b \]
\[ \boxed{\vec{BC}=\mathbf b-\mathbf a} \]
QUESTION 03 Foundation

The parallelogram

\(ABCD\) is a parallelogram. Given

\[ \vec{AB}=\mathbf a, \qquad \vec{AD}=\mathbf b, \]

find \(\vec{AC}\) in terms of \(\mathbf a\) and \(\mathbf b\).

Parallelogram \(ABCD\)
Solution

In a parallelogram, \(\vec{DC}=\vec{AB}\). Therefore we can travel from \(A\) to \(C\) using \(A\to D\) and \(D\to C\):

\[ \vec{AC} = \vec{AD}+\vec{DC} \]

Since \[ \vec{AD}=\mathbf b \] and \[ \vec{DC}=\vec{AB}=\mathbf a, \] we get

\[ \vec{AC} = \mathbf b+\mathbf a \]
\[ \boxed{\vec{AC}=\mathbf a+\mathbf b} \]
QUESTION 04 Ratios

Dividing a vector in a ratio

\(P\) lies on \(AB\) and divides the line segment in the ratio

\[ AP:PB=3:5. \]

If

\[ \vec{AB}=\mathbf a, \]

find \(\vec{AP}\).

\(AP:PB=3:5\)
Solution

The whole ratio contains \(3+5=8\) parts.

\(AP\) contains 3 of these 8 parts, so

\[ \vec{AP} = \frac{3}{8}\vec{AB} \]

Since \(\vec{AB}=\mathbf a\),

\[ \vec{AP} = \frac{3}{8}\mathbf a \]
\[ \boxed{\vec{AP}=\frac38\mathbf a} \]
QUESTION 05 Ratios

A ratio inside a parallelogram

\(ABCD\) is a parallelogram. Let

\[ \vec{AB}=\mathbf a, \qquad \vec{AD}=\mathbf b. \]

\(P\) lies on \(AB\) such that

\[ AP:PB=2:3. \]

Find \(\vec{PC}\) in terms of \(\mathbf a\) and \(\mathbf b\).

\(P\) divides \(AB\) in the ratio \(2:3\)
Solution

First find \(\vec{AP}\). Since \(AP:PB=2:3\),

\[ \vec{AP} = \frac{2}{5}\vec{AB} = \frac25\mathbf a \]

To travel from \(P\) to \(C\), travel from \(P\) to \(B\), then from \(B\) to \(C\).

\[ \vec{PC} = \vec{PB}+\vec{BC} \]

Since \(PB\) is the remaining \(3/5\) of \(AB\),

\[ \vec{PB} = \frac35\mathbf a \]

Also, because \(ABCD\) is a parallelogram, \[ \vec{BC}=\vec{AD}=\mathbf b. \] Therefore,

\[ \vec{PC} = \frac35\mathbf a+\mathbf b \]
\[ \boxed{\vec{PC}=\frac35\mathbf a+\mathbf b} \]
QUESTION 06 Ratios

Working backwards

\(P\) lies on \(AB\). The vectors satisfy

\[ \vec{AP}=\frac{3}{8}\mathbf a, \qquad \vec{AB}=\mathbf a. \]

Determine the ratio \(AP:PB\).

Solution

Since \[ \vec{AB}=\mathbf a \] and \[ \vec{AP}=\frac38\mathbf a, \] the segment \(AP\) represents \(3\) parts out of \(8\) parts of the whole segment \(AB\).

Therefore the remaining part \(PB\) represents \[ 8-3=5 \] parts.

\[ \boxed{AP:PB=3:5} \]
QUESTION 07 Geometry

A point on two sides

\(ABCD\) is a parallelogram with

\[ \vec{AB}=\mathbf a, \qquad \vec{AD}=\mathbf b. \]

\(P\) lies on \(AB\) such that

\[ AP:PB=1:2. \]

\(Q\) lies on \(AD\) such that

\[ AQ:QD=1:2. \]

Find \(\vec{PQ}\) in terms of \(\mathbf a\) and \(\mathbf b\).

Solution

Since \(AP:PB=1:2\), the point \(P\) is one-third of the way from \(A\) to \(B\).

\[ \vec{AP} = \frac13\mathbf a \]

Similarly, \(AQ:QD=1:2\), so

\[ \vec{AQ} = \frac13\mathbf b \]

To travel from \(P\) to \(Q\), go from \(P\) back to \(A\), then from \(A\) to \(Q\):

\[ \vec{PQ} = \vec{PA}+\vec{AQ} \]

Since \[ \vec{PA} = -\frac13\mathbf a, \] we obtain

\[ \vec{PQ} = -\frac13\mathbf a+\frac13\mathbf b \]
\[ \boxed{ \vec{PQ} = \frac13(\mathbf b-\mathbf a) } \]
QUESTION 08 Geometry

Expressing a diagonal

\(ABCD\) is a parallelogram. \(P\) lies on \(AB\) such that

\[ AP:PB=3:2. \]

Given

\[ \vec{AB}=\mathbf a, \qquad \vec{AD}=\mathbf b, \]

find \(\vec{PC}\) in terms of \(\mathbf a\) and \(\mathbf b\).

Solution

The ratio contains \(3+2=5\) parts. Therefore \(PB\) is \(2/5\) of \(AB\):

\[ \vec{PB} = \frac25\mathbf a \]

In the parallelogram, \[ \vec{BC}=\vec{AD}=\mathbf b. \]

Therefore,

\[ \vec{PC} = \vec{PB}+\vec{BC} \]
\[ \vec{PC} = \frac25\mathbf a+\mathbf b \]
\[ \boxed{ \vec{PC} = \frac25\mathbf a+\mathbf b } \]
QUESTION 09 Advanced

Two routes to the same point

\(ABCD\) is a parallelogram with

\[ \vec{AB}=\mathbf a, \qquad \vec{AD}=\mathbf b. \]

\(P\) lies on \(AB\) such that

\[ AP:PB=2:3. \]

Find \(\vec{PD}\) in terms of \(\mathbf a\) and \(\mathbf b\).

Solution

Since \(AP:PB=2:3\), the whole segment contains \(5\) parts. Therefore

\[ \vec{AP} = \frac25\mathbf a \]

To travel from \(P\) to \(D\), travel from \(P\) to \(A\), then from \(A\) to \(D\):

\[ \vec{PD} = \vec{PA}+\vec{AD} \]

Since \[ \vec{PA} = -\frac25\mathbf a \] and \[ \vec{AD}=\mathbf b, \] we get

\[ \vec{PD} = -\frac25\mathbf a+\mathbf b \]
\[ \boxed{ \vec{PD} = \mathbf b-\frac25\mathbf a } \]
QUESTION 10 Challenge

The final construction

\(ABCD\) is a parallelogram. Let

\[ \vec{AB}=\mathbf a, \qquad \vec{AD}=\mathbf b. \]

\(P\) lies on \(AB\) such that

\[ AP:PB=2:3. \]

\(Q\) lies on \(AD\) such that

\[ AQ:QD=3:2. \]

Find \(\vec{PQ}\) in terms of \(\mathbf a\) and \(\mathbf b\).

Then determine the vector \(\vec{CQ}\) in terms of \(\mathbf a\) and \(\mathbf b\).

Solution

First determine \(\vec{AP}\). Since \(AP:PB=2:3\),

\[ \vec{AP} = \frac25\mathbf a \]

Also, \(AQ:QD=3:2\), so the whole segment \(AD\) contains \(5\) parts and \(AQ\) contains \(3\) of them:

\[ \vec{AQ} = \frac35\mathbf b \]

To find \(\vec{PQ}\), travel from \(P\) back to \(A\), then from \(A\) to \(Q\):

\[ \vec{PQ} = \vec{PA}+\vec{AQ} \]

Since \[ \vec{PA} = -\frac25\mathbf a, \] we obtain

\[ \vec{PQ} = -\frac25\mathbf a + \frac35\mathbf b \]
\[ \boxed{ \vec{PQ} = \frac35\mathbf b-\frac25\mathbf a } \]

Now determine \(\vec{CQ}\). Travel from \(C\) to \(D\), then from \(D\) to \(Q\):

\[ \vec{CQ} = \vec{CD}+\vec{DQ} \]

Since \[ \vec{CD}=-\vec{AB}=-\mathbf a \] and \(Q\) divides \(AD\) in the ratio \(3:2\), \(DQ\) is \(2/5\) of \(DA\). Therefore

\[ \vec{DQ} = \frac25\vec{DA} = -\frac25\mathbf b \]

Hence

\[ \vec{CQ} = -\mathbf a-\frac25\mathbf b \]
\[ \boxed{ \vec{CQ} = -\mathbf a-\frac25\mathbf b } \]

Vector thinking, completed.

You have moved from direct vector relationships to ratios, parallelograms and multi-step geometric constructions. The important part is not memorising a route. It is choosing a route that makes the vectors work for you.
