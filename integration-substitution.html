Integration Using Substitution

A systematic method for simplifying complicated integrals by replacing part of an expression with a new variable.

CHOOSE u = g(x)
→
DIFFERENTIATE du = g′(x)dx
→
REWRITE ∫ f(u)du
01 / THE IDEA

What is substitution?

Sometimes an integral contains a complicated expression inside another function. Instead of integrating the expression directly, we can give that inner expression a new name.

This new variable is usually called u. The process is known as integration by substitution.

The main idea

Choose a useful expression as u, find du, rewrite the whole integral using u and du, integrate, and then substitute back.

u = g(x)      du = g′(x)dx
02 / THE METHOD

The substitution process

Keep these steps in order. The important point is that the integral must eventually be written completely in terms of u.

1

Choose u

Choose the expression that makes the integral simpler.

u = g(x)
2

Find du

Differentiate u with respect to x.

du = g′(x)dx
3

Rewrite the integral

Replace the chosen expression and differential with u and du.

4

Integrate with respect to u

Apply the appropriate integration rule.

5

Substitute back

Replace u with the original expression.

6

Simplify

Write the final answer clearly in terms of x.

03 / CHOOSING u

How do you choose u?

A useful substitution often comes from an expression that is inside another function, especially when its derivative also appears in the integral.

∫ 2x(x² + 3)4 dx

Here, x² + 3 is inside the fourth power and its derivative is 2x. This makes u = x² + 3 a natural choice.

04 / WORKED EXAMPLE 01

Power substitution

EXAMPLE 01

Evaluate the integral.

∫ 2x(x² + 3)4 dx
STEP 1 • CHOOSE u
u = x² + 3
STEP 2 • FIND du
du = 2x dx
STEP 3 • REWRITE
∫ 2x(x² + 3)4 dx = ∫ u4 du
STEP 4 • INTEGRATE
∫ u4 du = u5 5 + C
STEP 5 • SUBSTITUTE BACK
= (x² + 3)5 5 + C
∫ 2x(x² + 3)4 dx = (x² + 3)5 5 + C
05 / WORKED EXAMPLE 02

When a constant factor is needed

EXAMPLE 02

Evaluate the integral.

∫ x√(x² + 1) dx
STEP 1 • CHOOSE u
u = x² + 1
STEP 2 • FIND du
du = 2x dx

Therefore:

x dx = 1 2 du
STEP 3 • REWRITE
∫ x√(x² + 1) dx = 1 2 ∫ u1/2 du
STEP 4 • INTEGRATE
= 1 2 × u3/2 3/2 + C
= u3/2 3 + C
STEP 5 • SUBSTITUTE BACK
= (x² + 1)3/2 3 + C
∫ x√(x² + 1) dx = (x² + 1)3/2 3 + C
06 / WORKED EXAMPLE 03

A reciprocal power

EXAMPLE 03

Evaluate the integral.

∫ 3x² (x³ + 4)² dx
STEP 1 • CHOOSE u
u = x³ + 4
STEP 2 • FIND du
du = 3x² dx
STEP 3 • REWRITE
∫ 3x² (x³ + 4)² dx = ∫ u−2 du
STEP 4 • INTEGRATE
∫ u−2 du = −u−1 + C
STEP 5 • SUBSTITUTE BACK
= − 1 x³ + 4 + C
∫ 3x² (x³ + 4)² dx = − 1 x³ + 4 + C
07 / WORKED EXAMPLE 04

A more complicated inner expression

EXAMPLE 04

Evaluate the integral.

∫ (2x + 1)(x² + x + 5)³ dx
STEP 1 • CHOOSE u
u = x² + x + 5
STEP 2 • FIND du
du = (2x + 1) dx
STEP 3 • REWRITE
∫ (2x + 1)(x² + x + 5)³ dx = ∫ u³ du
STEP 4 • INTEGRATE
∫ u³ du = u⁴ 4 + C
STEP 5 • SUBSTITUTE BACK
= (x² + x + 5)⁴ 4 + C
∫ (2x + 1)(x² + x + 5)³ dx = (x² + x + 5)⁴ 4 + C
08 / WORKED EXAMPLE 05

Substitution leading to a logarithm

Substitution can turn a complicated fraction into the familiar logarithmic integral.

EXAMPLE 05

Evaluate the integral.

∫ 6x 3x² + 5 dx
STEP 1 • CHOOSE u
u = 3x² + 5
STEP 2 • FIND du
du = 6x dx
STEP 3 • REWRITE
∫ 6x 3x² + 5 dx = ∫ 1 u du
STEP 4 • INTEGRATE
∫ 1 u du = ln|u| + C
STEP 5 • SUBSTITUTE BACK
= ln|3x² + 5| + C
∫ 6x 3x² + 5 dx = ln|3x² + 5| + C
09 / DEFINITE INTEGRALS

Substitution with limits

For a definite integral, the limits can be changed from x-values into u-values. This lets the calculation remain completely in terms of u.

EXAMPLE 06

Evaluate the definite integral.

∫01 2x(x² + 1)³ dx
STEP 1 • CHOOSE u
u = x² + 1
STEP 2 • FIND du
du = 2x dx
STEP 3 • CHANGE THE LIMITS

When x = 0:

u = 0² + 1 = 1

When x = 1:

u = 1² + 1 = 2
STEP 4 • REWRITE
∫12 u³ du
STEP 5 • INTEGRATE
= [ u⁴ 4 ]12
STEP 6 • APPLY THE LIMITS
= 2⁴ 4 − 1⁴ 4
= 15 4
∫01 2x(x² + 1)³ dx = 15 4
10 / IMPORTANT DISTINCTION

Recognize the method

IMPORTANT

Substitution is a method, not a single formula.

Sometimes an integral can be recognized immediately. Other times, substitution makes the structure clear.

01

Derivative appears

If the integrand has the form f′(x) / f(x), the logarithmic rule may apply directly.

02

Inner expression repeats

If replacing an inner expression with u makes the integral simpler, substitution is a useful approach.

03

Another structure appears

If substitution does not simplify the integral, another integration method may be more appropriate.

11 / COMMON MISTAKES

Watch out for these

Choosing u without checking du Make sure the differential part of the integral can also be rewritten.
Leaving x behind After substitution, the integral should be completely expressed in u.
Forgetting a constant factor If du = 2x dx but the integral contains x dx, then x dx = 1/2 du.
Forgetting to substitute back For an indefinite integral, the final answer should normally be in x.
Forgetting to change definite limits If you change x into u, change the limits too.
QUICK SUMMARY

Substitution in one sequence

  • Choose a useful expression and call it u.
  • Differentiate to find du.
  • Rewrite the entire integral using u and du.
  • Integrate with respect to u.
  • Substitute back to x for an indefinite integral.
  • For definite integrals, change the limits if you keep the variable as u.
u = g(x)     du = g′(x)dx
