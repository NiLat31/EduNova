Integration Using Substitution

A systematic method for simplifying complicated integrals by replacing part of the expression with a new variable.

01 / THE IDEA

What is substitution?

Sometimes an integral contains a complicated expression repeated inside another function. Instead of integrating the expression directly, we can temporarily give that expression a new name.

This method is called integration by substitution. The new variable is usually written as u.

The main idea

Choose a part of the integral to become u. Then find du, rewrite the entire integral in terms of u, integrate, and finally substitute back to the original variable.

u = g(x)
du = g′(x)\,dx
02 / THE METHOD

The substitution process

There are six main steps. Keeping these steps in order makes the method much easier to follow.

1

Choose u

Choose the expression that makes the integral simpler.

u = g(x)
2

Find du

Differentiate your expression for u.

du = g′(x)\,dx
3

Rewrite the integral

Replace the chosen expression and the corresponding differential with u and du.

4

Integrate with respect to u

Once the integral is completely written in terms of u, integrate it normally.

5

Substitute back

Replace u with the original expression.

6

Simplify

Write the final answer clearly in terms of x.

03 / CHOOSING u

How do you choose u?

A good substitution usually turns a complicated part of the integral into a simple variable.

Look for an expression that is inside another function, especially when its derivative, or a constant multiple of its derivative, also appears elsewhere in the integral.

∫ 2x(x² + 3)4 dx

The expression x² + 3 is inside the fourth power, and its derivative is 2x.

Therefore, choosing u = x² + 3 makes the integral much simpler.

04 / WORKED EXAMPLE 01

Power substitution

EXAMPLE 01

Evaluate the integral.

∫ 2x(x² + 3)4 dx
STEP 1 • CHOOSE u

Choose the expression inside the power.

u = x² + 3
STEP 2 • FIND du
du = 2x dx
STEP 3 • REWRITE THE INTEGRAL

Since u = x² + 3 and du = 2x dx, the integral becomes:

∫ u4 du
STEP 4 • INTEGRATE
∫ u4 du = u5 5 + C
STEP 5 • SUBSTITUTE BACK
= (x² + 3)5 5 + C
∫ 2x(x² + 3)4 dx = (x² + 3)5 5 + C
05 / WORKED EXAMPLE 02

When a factor is missing

Sometimes the derivative of the chosen expression appears with a constant factor. We can account for that factor before integrating.

EXAMPLE 02

Evaluate the integral.

∫ x√(x² + 1) dx
STEP 1 • CHOOSE u
u = x² + 1
STEP 2 • FIND du
du = 2x dx

The integral contains x dx rather than 2x dx.

Divide both sides by 2:

x dx = 1 2 du
STEP 3 • REWRITE

Since √(x² + 1) = √u:

∫ x√(x² + 1) dx = 1 2 ∫ u1/2 du
STEP 4 • INTEGRATE
= 1 2 × u3/2 3/2 + C
= 1 3 u3/2 + C
STEP 5 • SUBSTITUTE BACK
= (x² + 1)3/2 3 + C
∫ x√(x² + 1) dx = (x² + 1)3/2 3 + C
06 / WORKED EXAMPLE 03

A reciprocal power

EXAMPLE 03

Evaluate the integral.

∫ 3x² (x³ + 4)² dx
STEP 1 • CHOOSE u
u = x³ + 4
STEP 2 • FIND du
du = 3x² dx
STEP 3 • REWRITE
∫ 3x² (x³ + 4)² dx = ∫ u−2 du
STEP 4 • INTEGRATE
∫ u−2 du = −u−1 + C
STEP 5 • SUBSTITUTE BACK
= −(x³ + 4)−1 + C
= − 1 x³ + 4 + C
∫ 3x² (x³ + 4)² dx = − 1 x³ + 4 + C
07 / WORKED EXAMPLE 04

A more complicated inner expression

EXAMPLE 04

Evaluate the integral.

∫ (2x + 1)(x² + x + 5)³ dx
STEP 1 • CHOOSE u
u = x² + x + 5
STEP 2 • FIND du
du = (2x + 1) dx
STEP 3 • REWRITE
∫ (2x + 1)(x² + x + 5)³ dx = ∫ u³ du
STEP 4 • INTEGRATE
∫ u³ du = u⁴ 4 + C
STEP 5 • SUBSTITUTE BACK
= (x² + x + 5)⁴ 4 + C
∫ (2x + 1)(x² + x + 5)³ dx = (x² + x + 5)⁴ 4 + C
08 / WORKED EXAMPLE 05

Substitution leading to a logarithm

Substitution can also lead to the logarithmic rule when the resulting integral contains 1/u.

EXAMPLE 05

Evaluate the integral.

∫ 6x 3x² + 5 dx
STEP 1 • CHOOSE u
u = 3x² + 5
STEP 2 • FIND du
du = 6x dx
STEP 3 • REWRITE
∫ 6x 3x² + 5 dx = ∫ 1 u du
STEP 4 • INTEGRATE
∫ 1 u du = ln|u| + C
STEP 5 • SUBSTITUTE BACK
= ln|3x² + 5| + C
∫ 6x 3x² + 5 dx = ln|3x² + 5| + C
09 / DEFINITE INTEGRALS

Substitution with limits

Substitution can also be used with definite integrals. In this case, there are two convenient approaches: substitute back to x before applying the limits, or change the limits from x-values to u-values.

Changing the limits allows the whole calculation to remain in terms of u.

EXAMPLE 06

Evaluate the definite integral.

∫01 2x(x² + 1)³ dx
STEP 1 • CHOOSE u
u = x² + 1
STEP 2 • FIND du
du = 2x dx
STEP 3 • CHANGE THE LIMITS

When x = 0:

u = 0² + 1 = 1

When x = 1:

u = 1² + 1 = 2

Therefore, the new limits are 1 and 2.

STEP 4 • REWRITE
∫12 u³ du
STEP 5 • INTEGRATE
= [ u⁴ 4 ]12
STEP 6 • APPLY THE LIMITS
= 2⁴ 4 − 1⁴ 4
= 16 − 1 4
= 15 4
∫01 2x(x² + 1)³ dx = 15 4
10 / IMPORTANT DISTINCTION

Substitution is a method, not a separate rule

It is important to recognize when substitution is useful. Some integrals can be solved immediately using a rule you already know, while others become much clearer after substitution.

01

Recognize the derivative

If the integrand already has the form f′(x) / f(x), the logarithmic rule may be applied directly.

02

Use substitution

If the integral becomes simpler after replacing a repeated inner expression with u, substitution is useful.

03

Choose another method

If no useful substitution simplifies the integral, another integration method may be more appropriate.

11 / COMMON MISTAKES

Watch out for these

Choosing u without checking du A substitution is only useful if the differential part of the integral can also be rewritten.
Leaving x behind After substitution, the integral should be completely expressed in terms of u and du.
Forgetting a constant factor If du = 2x dx but the integral contains x dx, remember that x dx = 1/2 du.
Forgetting to substitute back For an indefinite integral, the final answer should normally be written in terms of the original variable.
Forgetting to change definite limits If you change the variable to u, the limits must also be changed before evaluating the definite integral.
QUICK SUMMARY

Substitution in one sequence

  • Choose a useful expression and call it u.
  • Differentiate to find du.
  • Rewrite the entire integral using u and du.
  • Integrate with respect to u.
  • Substitute back to x for an indefinite integral.
  • For a definite integral, either substitute back or change the limits to u-values.
u = g(x)     du = g′(x)\,dx
