Practice Questions

Apply what you have learned about the Trapezium Rule. Work through each question carefully and reveal the solution when you are ready.

Keep in Mind

Before you begin, remember the key ideas from the lesson:

  • The number of intervals is n.
  • The width of each interval is h = (b − a) / n.
  • The first and last y-values are used once.
  • Every interior y-value is multiplied by 2.
  • The Trapezium Rule normally gives an approximation, so use ≈.

Overestimate or Underestimate?

The Trapezium Rule replaces the curved parts of a graph with straight-line segments.

If the trapezia lie mostly above the curve, the estimated area is greater than the true area. This is an overestimate.

If the trapezia lie mostly below the curve, the estimated area is less than the true area. This is an underestimate.

Quick check: Look at where the straight-line approximation lies compared with the curve.
Question 1
3 marks
Use the Trapezium Rule with four equal intervals to estimate the area under a curve between x = 0 and x = 4. The y-values are:
x 0 1 2 3 4
y 2 3 5 4 7
Solution
Step 1: Find h
h = 4 − 0 4 = 4 4 = 1
Step 2: Apply the formula
A ≈ 1 2 [2 + 7 + 2(3 + 5 + 4)]
Step 3: Calculate the interior values 3 + 5 + 4 = 12

2(12) = 24
Step 4: Calculate the quantity inside the brackets 2 + 7 + 24 = 33

Therefore:
A ≈ 33 2
Step 5: Calculate the final answer
A ≈ 33 2 = 16.5
A ≈ 16.5
Question 2
4 marks
The values of a function at five equally spaced points are shown below. Use the Trapezium Rule to estimate the area between x = 2 and x = 6.
x 2 3 4 5 6
y 4 6 7 9 8
Solution
Step 1: Find n The x-values are: 2, 3, 4, 5, 6

There are five points, so the number of intervals is one less than the number of points.
n = 5 − 1 = 4
Step 2: Find h
h = 6 − 2 4 = 4 4 = 1
Step 3: Apply the Trapezium Rule The first y-value is 4.
The last y-value is 8.
The interior y-values are 6, 7 and 9.
A ≈ 1 2 [4 + 8 + 2(6 + 7 + 9)]
Step 4: Calculate the interior values
6 + 7 + 9 = 22

2(22) = 44
Step 5: Calculate the quantity inside the brackets
4 + 8 + 44 = 56
Step 6: Calculate the final answer
A ≈ 56 2 = 28
A ≈ 28

Question 6
3 marks
A curve is concave upward throughout an interval. The Trapezium Rule is used to estimate the area under the curve.

Is the Trapezium Rule likely to give an overestimate or an underestimate? Explain your answer.
Solution
Step 1: Consider the shape of the curve A concave-up curve bends upward.

When the Trapezium Rule joins points on this curve with straight-line segments, those straight lines lie above the curve.
Step 2: Compare the estimated area with the true area Because the trapezia include the region between the straight-line segments and the curve, the calculated area is larger than the actual area under the curve.
Overestimate
Question 7
3 marks
A curve is concave downward throughout an interval. The Trapezium Rule is used to estimate the area under the curve.

Is the result likely to be an overestimate or an underestimate? Explain why.
Solution
Step 1: Consider the shape of the curve A concave-down curve bends downward.

When the Trapezium Rule joins points on this curve with straight-line segments, those straight lines lie below the curve.
Step 2: Compare the estimated area with the true area Because the trapezia cover less area than the actual region under the curve, the calculated area is smaller than the true area.
Underestimate
Question 8
5 marks
A student uses the Trapezium Rule to estimate the area under a curve. They divide the interval into 4 equal intervals and obtain an estimate of 24.8 square units. The graph shows that the straight-line approximation lies above the curve throughout the interval.

(a) Is the estimate an overestimate or an underestimate?
(b) Is the true area greater than or less than 24.8 square units?
(c) Explain what would generally happen to the accuracy if the number of intervals were increased.
Solution
(a) Determine whether the estimate is an overestimate or underestimate. The straight-line approximation lies above the curve throughout the interval.

Therefore, the trapezia cover more area than the actual area under the curve.
Overestimate
(b) Compare the estimate with the true area. Since the estimate is an overestimate:

Estimated area > True area

The estimated area is 24.8 square units. Therefore:
24.8 > True area
So:
True area < 24.8 square units
(c) Consider what happens when the number of intervals increases. Increasing the number of intervals makes each trapezium narrower.

Narrower trapezia allow the straight-line approximation to follow the curve more closely.

Therefore, the difference between the estimated area and the true area generally becomes smaller.

Hence, the Trapezium Rule generally becomes more accurate when the number of intervals is increased.
