COMPLEX NUMBERS · TOPIC 05

Complex Roots of Polynomial Equations.

Discover what happens when polynomial equations have complex solutions. Learn why non-real roots appear in conjugate pairs and explore the possible roots of cubic and quartic equations.

z
01 · UNDERSTANDING THE IDEA

How many roots does an equation have?

Before exploring complex roots, let's recall something important about polynomial equations.

The degree of a polynomial tells us how many roots the equation has, when complex roots are included and repeated roots are counted according to their multiplicity.

THE GENERAL RULE
\[ \boxed{\text{Degree }n \Longrightarrow n\text{ roots}} \]

Consider these examples:

DEGREE 2

Quadratic equation

Has two roots.

\[ ax^2+bx+c=0 \]
DEGREE 3

Cubic equation

Has three roots.

\[ ax^3+bx^2+cx+d=0 \]
DEGREE 4

Quartic equation

Has four roots.

\[ ax^4+bx^3+cx^2+dx+e=0 \]
DEGREE 5

Quintic equation

Has five roots.

\[ ax^5+\cdots=0 \]

Key information

Every polynomial equation of degree \(n\) has exactly \(n\) complex roots, counting repeated roots. Some of these roots may be real, while others may be non-real.

02 · REAL AND COMPLEX SOLUTIONS

What if there are no real roots?

You have previously studied quadratic equations using the discriminant:

\[ \Delta=b^2-4ac \]

The discriminant helps us determine the nature of the roots:

Three possible situations

1. If \(\Delta>0\)

There are two distinct real roots.

2. If \(\Delta=0\)

There is one repeated real root.

3. If \(\Delta<0\)

There are two non-real complex roots.

For example, consider:

\[ x^2+4=0 \] \[ x^2=-4 \] \[ x=\pm 2i \]

This equation has no real solutions. However, it still has two complex roots:

\[ \boxed{x=2i\quad\text{and}\quad x=-2i} \]

Remember

A root does not have to be real. Complex numbers allow us to include solutions that cannot be expressed using real numbers alone.

03 · AN IMPORTANT PROPERTY

Complex roots come in pairs.

Here is one of the most important properties of polynomial equations with real coefficients.

The conjugate root rule

If a polynomial equation has real coefficients and \(a+bi\) is a non-real root, then \(a-bi\) must also be a root.

Remember that these two complex numbers are conjugates:

\[ z=a+bi \] \[ z^*=a-bi \]

Notice that the real part stays the same, while the sign of the imaginary part changes.

AN EXAMPLE OF CONJUGATE ROOTS
\[ 2+3i \qquad\longleftrightarrow\qquad 2-3i \]

Therefore, if you are told that one root is \(2+3i\), you immediately know that the other root is \(2-3i\).

WORKED EXAMPLE 01

Find the missing complex root.

A polynomial equation with real coefficients has \(2-5i\) as one of its roots. Find its conjugate root.

SOLUTION
The real part is \(2\).
The imaginary part is \(-5i\).
Change the sign of the imaginary part.
\[ \boxed{2+5i} \]

Therefore, the missing root is \(2+5i\).

Keep in mind

This conjugate-pair rule applies when the polynomial has real coefficients. It does not automatically apply to polynomials with non-real coefficients.

04 · UNDERSTANDING WHY

Why do conjugate roots appear together?

Let's understand this using a simple example.

Consider the quadratic equation:

\[ x^2-4x+13=0 \]

Using the quadratic formula:

\[ x=\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a} \]

Substitute \(a=1\), \(b=-4\), and \(c=13\):

\[ x=\frac{4\pm\sqrt{16-52}}{2} \] \[ x=\frac{4\pm\sqrt{-36}}{2} \] \[ x=\frac{4\pm6i}{2} \] \[ \boxed{x=2\pm3i} \]

We obtain two roots:

\[ \boxed{2+3i\quad\text{and}\quad2-3i} \]

The two roots have the same real part and opposite imaginary parts.

Remember

Whenever you find a non-real root of a polynomial with real coefficients, you can immediately identify its conjugate without solving the equation again.

05 · CUBIC EQUATIONS

Possible roots of a cubic equation.

A cubic equation has degree three, so it has three roots in total, counting multiplicity.

Since non-real complex roots come in conjugate pairs, a cubic equation with real coefficients can have the following possibilities:

POSSIBILITY 01

Three real roots

\[ 1,\quad 2,\quad 3 \]

All three roots are real.

POSSIBILITY 02

One real root and one conjugate pair

\[ 1,\quad 2+3i,\quad 2-3i \]

One real root and two non-real roots.

WORKED EXAMPLE 02

Find the remaining roots.

A cubic equation with real coefficients has one root equal to \(1\) and another root equal to \(3+2i\). Find its third root.

SOLUTION
The equation is cubic, so it has three roots.
One root is \(1\).
Another root is \(3+2i\).
Since the coefficients are real, the conjugate \(3-2i\) must also be a root.
\[ \boxed{1,\quad 3+2i,\quad 3-2i} \]

These are all three roots of the cubic equation.

06 · QUARTIC EQUATIONS

Possible roots of a quartic equation.

A quartic equation has degree four, meaning it has four roots in total, counting multiplicity.

There are three possible combinations when its coefficients are real:

POSSIBILITY 01

Four real roots

\[ 1,\quad2,\quad3,\quad4 \]

All four roots are real.

POSSIBILITY 02

Two real roots and one pair

\[ 1,\quad2,\quad3+i,\quad3-i \]

Two real roots and two non-real roots.

POSSIBILITY 03

Two pairs of complex roots

\[ 1+i,\quad1-i \] \[ 2+3i,\quad2-3i \]

All four roots are non-real.

Keep in mind

A quartic equation with real coefficients cannot have exactly one non-real root or exactly three non-real roots. Non-real roots must occur in pairs.

07 · IMPORTANT DETAILS

Common mistakes to avoid.

1. Changing the real part

The conjugate of \(4+7i\) is \(4-7i\), not \(-4-7i\).

2. Forgetting the coefficients must be real

The conjugate-pair rule is guaranteed for polynomials with real coefficients.

3. Forgetting the total number of roots

A cubic equation has three roots, not two. A quartic equation has four roots, not three. Remember to count all roots, including repeated ones.

4. Confusing real roots with non-real roots

A complex number can have a zero imaginary part. For example, \(5+0i=5\) is real.

08 · LESSON RECAP

What have we learned?

The essential facts

  • A polynomial equation of degree \(n\) has \(n\) complex roots, counting multiplicity.
  • Some roots may be real, while others may be non-real.
  • For polynomials with real coefficients, non-real complex roots come in conjugate pairs.
  • A cubic has either three real roots or one real root and one conjugate pair.
  • A quartic can have four real roots, two real roots and one pair, or two conjugate pairs.
