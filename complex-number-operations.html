Operations with
Complex Numbers

Build a reliable method for combining, expanding and dividing complex numbers. The key is to keep real and imaginary parts organised, and remember what happens when \(i\) is squared.

Start here

One form. Two parts.

Every complex number can be written as \(a+bi\), where \(a\) and \(b\) are real numbers and \(i^2=-1\).

\[\boxed{z=a+bi=\underbrace{a}_{\text{real part}}+\underbrace{b}_{\text{imaginary coefficient}}i}\]

Real part

\(\operatorname{Re}(z)=a\). It is the number without \(i\).

Imaginary coefficient

\(\operatorname{Im}(z)=b\). It is the coefficient of \(i\), not \(bi\) itself.

Our target form: unless a question says otherwise, simplify your final answer into \(a+bi\). Keep the real term first and the \(i\)-term second.

For example, \(4-3i\) has real part \(4\) and imaginary coefficient \(-3\). The minus sign belongs to the coefficient.

01

Addition and subtraction

Combine like parts, just as you do with algebraic terms.

Think of a complex number as having two separate lanes: a real lane and an \(i\)-lane. Add or subtract within each lane. Do not mix the lanes.

\[\boxed{(a+bi)\pm(c+di)=(a\pm c)+(b\pm d)i}\]
Reliable routine
Remove brackets carefully → collect the real terms → collect the coefficients of \(i\) → write the answer in \(a+bi\) form.
Worked example 1 · Addition
Simplify \((7+4i)+(2-9i)\).
\[\begin{aligned}(7+4i)+(2-9i)&=(7+2)+(4i-9i)\\&=9-5i\end{aligned}\]

The real parts give \(9\), while the imaginary terms give \(-5i\).

Worked example 2 · Subtraction
Simplify \((5-3i)-(8+6i)\).
Open the step-by-step solution

Step 1: The minus sign changes both terms in the second bracket.

\[5-3i-8-6i\]

Step 2: Collect the real and imaginary terms.

\[(5-8)+(-3-6)i=\boxed{-3-9i}\]

Be especially careful not to change only the first sign inside the second bracket.

Common slip: \((a+bi)-(c+di)\) is \(a+bi-c-di\), not \(a+bi-c+di\). The subtraction applies to the whole bracket.
02

Multiplication

Expand first. Simplify powers of \(i\) afterwards.

Multiplication follows the distributive law. Each term in the first bracket multiplies each term in the second bracket. Once expanded, use the identity below.

\[\boxed{i^2=-1}\]

That identity turns the product of two imaginary terms into a real term:

\[(bi)(di)=bd\,i^2=-bd\]
Four-product method
Real × real, real × imaginary, imaginary × real, imaginary × imaginary. Then replace \(i^2\) with \(-1\) and collect like terms.
Worked example 3 · Two brackets
Simplify \((3+2i)(4-5i)\).
Step-by-step solution

Expand all four products:

\[\begin{aligned}(3+2i)(4-5i)&=12-15i+8i-10i^2\\&=12-7i-10(-1)\\&=\boxed{22-7i}\end{aligned}\]

Notice the last product is \(-10i^2=+10\). This is where the real part changes.

Worked example 4 · A square
Simplify \((2-3i)^2\).
\[\begin{aligned}(2-3i)^2&=(2-3i)(2-3i)\\&=4-6i-6i+9i^2\\&=4-12i-9\\&=\boxed{-5-12i}\end{aligned}\]

A square means the bracket is multiplied by itself. Do not square each term separately.

Quick check: after expanding, the \(i^2\)-term must become real. Your final answer should contain no \(i^2\), \(i^3\), or higher powers of \(i\).
03

The complex conjugate

A sign change that creates a useful real result.

The conjugate of \(a+bi\) is \(a-bi\). The real part stays exactly the same, while the sign of the imaginary term changes.

\[\boxed{(a+bi)^*=a-bi}\]

Number

\[z=6+5i\]

Conjugate

\[z^*=6-5i\]

The special feature is what happens when a number is multiplied by its conjugate:

\[\begin{aligned}(a+bi)(a-bi)&=a^2-abi+abi-b^2i^2\\&=a^2+b^2\end{aligned}\]
Important result: \(zz^*=a^2+b^2\), which is always real and non-negative. The middle terms cancel, and \(i^2=-1\).
Worked example 5 · Conjugate product
Find \((4+3i)(4-3i)\).
\[\begin{aligned}&=16-12i+12i-9i^2\\&=16+9\\&=\boxed{25}\end{aligned}\]

The imaginary terms cancel completely.

04

Division

Make the denominator real by using its conjugate.

Dividing directly by a complex number usually leaves \(i\) in the denominator. We avoid that by multiplying the numerator and denominator by the conjugate of the denominator. This does not change the value of the fraction, because we multiply by a form of \(1\).

\[\boxed{\frac{a+bi}{c+di}\times\frac{c-di}{c-di}}\]

The denominator becomes:

\[(c+di)(c-di)=c^2+d^2\]
Division method
1. Identify the denominator. 2. Write its conjugate. 3. Multiply top and bottom by that conjugate. 4. Expand and use \(i^2=-1\). 5. Collect the result into \(x+yi\) form.
Worked example 6 · Division
Write \(\dfrac{5+i}{2-3i}\) in the form \(x+yi\).
Step-by-step solution

Step 1: The denominator is \(2-3i\), so its conjugate is \(2+3i\).

Step 2: Multiply the numerator and denominator by \(2+3i\).

\[\frac{5+i}{2-3i}\times\frac{2+3i}{2+3i}\]

Step 3: Expand the numerator and denominator separately.

\[\begin{aligned}\text{Numerator}&=(5+i)(2+3i)\\&=10+15i+2i+3i^2\\&=7+17i\\[3pt]\text{Denominator}&=(2-3i)(2+3i)\\&=4+9=13\end{aligned}\]

Step 4: Divide each term by \(13\).

\[\boxed{\frac{5+i}{2-3i}=\frac7{13}+\frac{17}{13}i}\]
Do not do this: multiplying only the denominator by its conjugate changes the value of the fraction. Whatever you do to the denominator, you must also do to the numerator.
Worked example 7 · A negative imaginary denominator
Simplify \(\dfrac{3-2i}{1+i}\).
\[\begin{aligned}\frac{3-2i}{1+i}\times\frac{1-i}{1-i} &=\frac{(3-2i)(1-i)}{(1+i)(1-i)}\\ &=\frac{3-3i-2i+2i^2}{1-i^2}\\ &=\frac{1-5i}{2}\\ &=\boxed{\frac12-\frac52i}\end{aligned}\]

b. \(z_1z_2\)

LEVEL 3 · APPLY
6

Find the unknowns

Given \((a+2i)+(3-bi)=7-4i\), where \(a,b\) are real, find \(a\) and \(b\).

Show solution

Collect the parts: \((a+3)+(2-b)i=7-4i\).

Equate real parts: \(a+3=7\), so \(a=4\).

Equate imaginary coefficients: \(2-b=-4\), so \(b=6\).

LEVEL 4 · CHALLENGE
7

A quotient with a parameter

Given \(\dfrac{a+bi}{1-2i}=3+i\), where \(a,b\) are real, find \(a\) and \(b\).

Show solution

Multiply both sides by \(1-2i\):

\(a+bi=(3+i)(1-2i)=3-6i+i-2i^2=5-5i\).

Therefore, \(a=5\) and \(b=-5\).

LEVEL 4 · CHALLENGE
8

Build an expression

Find the complex number \(z\) if \(2z-(3-4i)=5+6i\). Give your answer in \(x+yi\) form.

Show solution

Add \(3-4i\) to both sides: \(2z=8+2i\).

Divide both parts by \(2\): \(\boxed{z=4+i}\).

LEVEL 4 · EXTENSION
9

Prove the result is real

For \(z=a+bi\), where \(a,b\) are real, show that \(z+z^*\) and \(zz^*\) are both real numbers.

Show solution

\(z+\overline z=(a+bi)+(a-bi)=2a\), which is real.

\(z\overline z=(a+bi)(a-bi)=a^2+b^2\), which is also real.
