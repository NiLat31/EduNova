FOUNDATION 01
Evaluate \[ \int x e^x\,dx. \]
FULL SOLUTION
Let \[ u=x,\qquad dv=e^x\,dx. \]
Therefore \[ du=dx \] and \[ v=e^x. \]
Using \[ \int u\,dv=uv-\int v\,du, \] we get \[ \int xe^x\,dx = xe^x-\int e^x\,dx. \]
Hence \[ \int xe^x\,dx = xe^x-e^x+C. \]
\[ \boxed{e^x(x-1)+C} \]
FOUNDATION 02
Evaluate \[ \int x\cos x\,dx. \]
FULL SOLUTION
Choose \[ u=x,\qquad dv=\cos x\,dx. \]
Then \[ du=dx,\qquad v=\sin x. \]
Therefore \[ \int x\cos x\,dx = x\sin x-\int\sin x\,dx. \]
Since \[ \int\sin x\,dx=-\cos x, \] we obtain \[ x\sin x+\cos x+C. \]
\[ \boxed{x\sin x+\cos x+C} \]
FOUNDATION 03
Evaluate \[ \int x\ln x\,dx. \]
FULL SOLUTION
Choose \[ u=\ln x,\qquad dv=x\,dx. \]
Differentiate \(u\): \[ du=\frac1x\,dx. \]
Integrate \(dv\): \[ v=\frac{x^2}{2}. \]
Apply integration by parts: \[ \int x\ln x\,dx = \frac{x^2}{2}\ln x - \int \frac{x^2}{2}\cdot\frac1x\,dx. \]
Simplify: \[ = \frac{x^2}{2}\ln x - \frac12\int x\,dx. \]
Hence \[ = \frac{x^2}{2}\ln x - \frac{x^2}{4} +C. \]
\[ \boxed{\frac{x^2}{2}\ln x-\frac{x^2}{4}+C} \]
FOUNDATION 04
Evaluate \[ \int xe^{2x}\,dx. \]
FULL SOLUTION
Let \[ u=x,\qquad dv=e^{2x}\,dx. \]
Then \[ du=dx \] and \[ v=\frac12e^{2x}. \]
Therefore \[ \int xe^{2x}\,dx = \frac{x e^{2x}}2 - \frac12\int e^{2x}\,dx. \]
Since \[ \int e^{2x}\,dx=\frac12e^{2x}, \] we get \[ \int xe^{2x}\,dx = \frac{x e^{2x}}2 - \frac{e^{2x}}4+C. \]
\[ \boxed{\frac{e^{2x}}4(2x-1)+C} \]
FOUNDATION 05
Evaluate \[ \int x^2e^x\,dx. \]
FULL SOLUTION
Choose \[ u=x^2,\qquad dv=e^x\,dx. \]
Then \[ du=2x\,dx,\qquad v=e^x. \]
Therefore \[ \int x^2e^x\,dx = x^2e^x-\int2xe^x\,dx. \]
We now evaluate \[ \int xe^x\,dx. \] Let \[ u=x,\qquad dv=e^x\,dx. \]
Thus \[ du=dx,\qquad v=e^x. \]
So \[ \int xe^x\,dx = xe^x-\int e^x\,dx = xe^x-e^x. \]
Substitute: \[ \int x^2e^x\,dx = x^2e^x - 2(xe^x-e^x)+C. \]
Therefore \[ = x^2e^x-2xe^x+2e^x+C. \]
\[ \boxed{e^x(x^2-2x+2)+C} \]

FULL SOLUTION

Hence \[ \int x^2\cos x\,dx = x^2\sin x - 2\int x\sin x\,dx. \]
For \[ \int x\sin x\,dx, \] choose \[ u=x,\qquad dv=\sin x\,dx. \]
Thus \[ du=dx,\qquad v=-\cos x. \]
Therefore \[ \int x\sin x\,dx = -x\cos x+\int\cos x\,dx = -x\cos x+\sin x. \]
Substitute: \[ \int x^2\cos x\,dx = x^2\sin x - 2(-x\cos x+\sin x)+C. \]
\[ \boxed{x^2\sin x+2x\cos x-2\sin x+C} \]

Thus \[ = x\ln x-\int1\,dx. \]

QUESTION 11
Evaluate the indefinite integral: \[\int x^2 e^{-x/2} \, dx\]
STEP-BY-STEP SOLUTION
Step 1: First Integration by Parts
Let \(u = x^2 \implies du = 2x \, dx\)
Let \(dv = e^{-x/2} \, dx \implies v = -2e^{-x/2}\)
Applying the formula: \[\int x^2 e^{-x/2} \, dx = -2x^2 e^{-x/2} - \int (-2e^{-x/2})(2x) \, dx\] \[= -2x^2 e^{-x/2} + 4\int x e^{-x/2} \, dx\]
Step 2: Second Integration by Parts
For the remaining integral \(\int x e^{-x/2} \, dx\):
Let \(u = x \implies du = dx\)
Let \(dv = e^{-x/2} \, dx \implies v = -2e^{-x/2}\)
\[\int x e^{-x/2} \, dx = -2x e^{-x/2} - \int -2e^{-x/2} \, dx\] \[= -2x e^{-x/2} - 4e^{-x/2}\]
Step 3: Combine and Distribute
Substitute the result back into the main expression: \[-2x^2 e^{-x/2} + 4\left(-2x e^{-x/2} - 4e^{-x/2}\right)\] \[= -2x^2 e^{-x/2} - 8x e^{-x/2} - 16e^{-x/2} + C\]
Final Answer: \(-2e^{-x/2}(x^2 + 4x + 8) + C\)
INTERMEDIATE 12
Evaluate \[ \int x^2\sin x\,dx. \]
FULL SOLUTION
Choose \[ u=x^2,\qquad dv=\sin x\,dx. \]
Then \[ du=2x\,dx,\qquad v=-\cos x. \]
Therefore \[ \int x^2\sin x\,dx = -x^2\cos x + 2\int x\cos x\,dx. \]
Now \[ \int x\cos x\,dx = x\sin x-\int\sin x\,dx. \]
Since \[ \int\sin x\,dx=-\cos x, \] we have \[ \int x\cos x\,dx = x\sin x+\cos x. \]
Therefore \[ \int x^2\sin x\,dx = -x^2\cos x + 2x\sin x + 2\cos x+C. \]
\[ \boxed{-x^2\cos x+2x\sin x+2\cos x+C} \]
QUESTION 13
Evaluate the indefinite integral: \[\int x^2 \cos(2x) \, dx\]
STEP-BY-STEP SOLUTION
Step 1: First Integration by Parts
Let \(u = x^2 \implies du = 2x \, dx\)
Let \(dv = \cos(2x) \, dx \implies v = \frac{1}{2}\sin(2x)\)
\[\int x^2 \cos(2x) \, dx = \frac{1}{2}x^2 \sin(2x) - \int \left(\frac{1}{2}\sin(2x)\right)(2x) \, dx\] \[= \frac{1}{2}x^2 \sin(2x) - \int x \sin(2x) \, dx\]
Step 2: Second Integration by Parts
For \(\int x \sin(2x) \, dx\):
Let \(u = x \implies du = dx\)
Let \(dv = \sin(2x) \, dx \implies v = -\frac{1}{2}\cos(2x)\)
\[\int x \sin(2x) \, dx = -\frac{1}{2}x \cos(2x) - \int \left(-\frac{1}{2}\cos(2x)\right) \, dx\] \[= -\frac{1}{2}x \cos(2x) + \frac{1}{4}\sin(2x)\]
Step 3: Distribute Negative Sign
Combine carefully ensuring proper sign distribution: \[\frac{1}{2}x^2 \sin(2x) - \left(-\frac{1}{2}x \cos(2x) + \frac{1}{4}\sin(2x)\right) + C\] \[= \frac{1}{2}x^2 \sin(2x) + \frac{1}{2}x \cos(2x) - \frac{1}{4}\sin(2x) + C\]
Final Answer: \(\frac{1}{2}x^2 \sin(2x) + \frac{1}{2}x \cos(2x) - \frac{1}{4}\sin(2x) + C\)
INTERMEDIATE 14
Evaluate \[ \int x\ln(1+x)\,dx. \]
FULL SOLUTION
Choose \[ u=\ln(1+x),\qquad dv=x\,dx. \]
Then \[ du=\frac{1}{1+x}\,dx, \qquad v=\frac{x^2}{2}. \]
Therefore \[ \int x\ln(1+x)\,dx = \frac{x^2}{2}\ln(1+x) - \frac12 \int\frac{x^2}{1+x}\,dx. \]
Divide: \[ \frac{x^2}{x+1} = x-1+\frac1{x+1}. \]
Hence \[ \int\frac{x^2}{1+x}\,dx = \int \left( x-1+\frac1{x+1} \right)dx. \]
Therefore \[ = \frac{x^2}{2}-x+\ln(1+x). \]
Substitute: \[ \int x\ln(1+x)\,dx = \frac{x^2}{2}\ln(1+x) - \frac12 \left( \frac{x^2}{2}-x+\ln(1+x) \right)+C. \]
\[ \boxed{ \frac{x^2-1}{2}\ln(1+x) -\frac{x^2}{4} +\frac{x}{2} +C } \]
INTERMEDIATE 15
Evaluate \[ \int_0^1 x e^x\,dx. \]
FULL SOLUTION
Choose \[ u=x,\qquad dv=e^x\,dx. \]
Then \[ du=dx,\qquad v=e^x. \]
Therefore \[ \int_0^1xe^x\,dx = \left[xe^x-e^x\right]_0^1. \]

At \(x=0\): \[ 0(e^0)-e^0=-1. \]
Hence \[ 0-(-1)=1. \]

Then \[ du=2x\,dx, \qquad v=\frac12e^{2x}. \]
Therefore \[ \int x^2e^{2x}\,dx = \frac{x^2e^{2x}}2 - \int xe^{2x}\,dx. \]
For the remaining integral, choose \[ u=x,\qquad dv=e^{2x}\,dx. \]
Thus \[ du=dx,\qquad v=\frac12e^{2x}. \]
Hence \[ \int xe^{2x}\,dx = \frac{xe^{2x}}2 - \frac12\int e^{2x}\,dx. \]
Since \[ \int e^{2x}\,dx=\frac12e^{2x}, \] we get \[ \int xe^{2x}\,dx = \frac{xe^{2x}}2-\frac{e^{2x}}4. \]
Therefore \[ \int x^2e^{2x}\,dx = \frac{x^2e^{2x}}2 - \frac{xe^{2x}}2 + \frac{e^{2x}}4+C. \]

QUESTION 17
Evaluate the indefinite integral: \[\int x^5 e^{x^3} \, dx\]
STEP-BY-STEP SOLUTION
Step 1: Algebraic Substitution First
Rewrite the integral as \(\int x^3 \cdot e^{x^3} \cdot x^2 \, dx\).
Let \(t = x^3 \implies dt = 3x^2 \, dx \implies x^2 \, dx = \frac{1}{3}dt\).
The integral transforms to: \[\frac{1}{3}\int t e^t \, dt\]
Step 2: Integration by Parts
For \(\int t e^t \, dt\):
Let \(u = t \implies du = dt\)
Let \(dv = e^t \, dt \implies v = e^t\)
\[\int t e^t \, dt = t e^t - \int e^t \, dt = t e^t - e^t\]
Step 3: Back-substitute and Scale
Multiply by the scaling factor \(\frac{1}{3}\) and replace \(t\) with \(x^3\): \[\frac{1}{3}(t e^t - e^t) = \frac{1}{3}x^3 e^{x^3} - \frac{1}{3}e^{x^3} + C\]
Final Answer: \(\frac{1}{3}e^{x^3}(x^3 - 1) + C\)
ADVANCED 18
Evaluate \[ \int e^x\sin x\,dx. \]
FULL SOLUTION
Let \[ I=\int e^x\sin x\,dx. \]
Choose \[ u=\sin x,\qquad dv=e^x\,dx. \]
Then \[ du=\cos x\,dx,\qquad v=e^x. \]
Therefore \[ I=e^x\sin x-\int e^x\cos x\,dx. \]
Let \[ J=\int e^x\cos x\,dx. \] Using integration by parts again: \[ u=\cos x,\qquad dv=e^x\,dx. \]
Thus \[ du=-\sin x\,dx,\qquad v=e^x. \]
Hence \[ J=e^x\cos x+\int e^x\sin x\,dx. \]
Since \[ I=e^x\sin x-J, \] substitute \(J\): \[ I=e^x\sin x-e^x\cos x-I. \]
Add \(I\) to both sides: \[ 2I=e^x(\sin x-\cos x). \]
Divide by \(2\): \[ I=\frac12e^x(\sin x-\cos x)+C. \]
\[ \boxed{ \frac{e^x}{2}(\sin x-\cos x)+C } \]
QUESTION 19
Evaluate the recurring integral: \[\int e^x \cos x \, dx\]
STEP-BY-STEP SOLUTION
Step 1: First Parts Pass
Let \(I = \int e^x \cos x \, dx\).
Let \(u = \cos x \implies du = -\sin x \, dx\)
Let \(dv = e^x \, dx \implies v = e^x\)
\[I = e^x \cos x + \int e^x \sin x \, dx\]
Step 2: Second Parts Pass
For \(\int e^x \sin x \, dx\):
Let \(u = \sin x \implies du = \cos x \, dx\)
Let \(dv = e^x \, dx \implies v = e^x\)
\[\int e^x \sin x \, dx = e^x \sin x - \int e^x \cos x \, dx = e^x \sin x - I\]
Step 3: Collect terms of \(I\)
Substitute back into the equation: \[I = e^x \cos x + e^x \sin x - I\] \[2I = e^x(\sin x + \cos x) \implies I = \frac{1}{2}e^x(\sin x + \cos x) + C\]
Final Answer: \(\frac{1}{2}e^x(\sin x + \cos x) + C\)
ADVANCED 20
Evaluate the definite integral: \[\int_{0}^{\pi} x^2 \sin(2x) \, dx\]
FULL SOLUTION
Step 1: First Integration by Parts Pass
Let \(u = x^2 \implies du = 2x \, dx\)
Let \(dv = \sin(2x) \, dx \implies v = -\frac{1}{2}\cos(2x)\)
Applying the formula: \[\int x^2 \sin(2x) \, dx = -\frac{1}{2}x^2 \cos(2x) - \int \left(-\frac{1}{2}\cos(2x)\right)(2x) \, dx\] \[= -\frac{1}{2}x^2 \cos(2x) + \int x \cos(2x) \, dx\]
Step 2: Second Integration by Parts Pass
For the remaining integral \(\int x \cos(2x) \, dx\):
Let \(u = x \implies du = dx\)
Let \(dv = \cos(2x) \, dx \implies v = \frac{1}{2}\sin(2x)\)
\[\int x \cos(2x) \, dx = \frac{1}{2}x \sin(2x) - \int \frac{1}{2}\sin(2x) \, dx\] \[= \frac{1}{2}x \sin(2x) + \frac{1}{4}\cos(2x)\]
Step 3: Combine and Apply Limits
Combining the two passes yields the general antiderivative: \[F(x) = -\frac{1}{2}x^2 \cos(2x) + \frac{1}{2}x \sin(2x) + \frac{1}{4}\cos(2x)\] Now evaluate from \(0\) to \(\pi\): \[\left[ -\frac{1}{2}x^2 \cos(2x) + \frac{1}{2}x \sin(2x) + \frac{1}{4}\cos(2x) \right]_{0}^{\pi}\]
Step 4: Upper Limit (\(x = \pi\))
Recall that \(\cos(2\pi) = 1\) and \(\sin(2\pi) = 0\): \[\left(-\frac{1}{2}\pi^2(1)\right) + \left(\frac{1}{2}\pi(0)\right) + \frac{1}{4}(1) = -\frac{\pi^2}{2} + \frac{1}{4}\]
Step 5: Lower Limit (\(x = 0\))
Recall that \(\cos(0) = 1\) and \(\sin(0) = 0\): \[\left(-\frac{1}{2}(0)^2(1)\right) + \left(\frac{1}{2}(0)(0)\right) + \frac{1}{4}(1) = \frac{1}{4}\]
Step 6: Subtract Limits
\[\left(-\frac{\pi^2}{2} + \frac{1}{4}\right) - \left(\frac{1}{4}\right) = -\frac{\pi^2}{2}\]
\[\boxed{-\frac{\pi^2}{2}}\]

Evaluate \[ \int x^2\ln(1+x^2)\,dx. \]
FULL SOLUTION
Choose \[ u=\ln(1+x^2), \qquad dv=x^2\,dx. \]
Then \[ du=\frac{2x}{1+x^2}\,dx, \qquad v=\frac{x^3}{3}. \]
Therefore \[ \int x^2\ln(1+x^2)\,dx = \frac{x^3}{3}\ln(1+x^2) - \frac23 \int \frac{x^4}{1+x^2}\,dx. \]
Divide: \[ \frac{x^4}{1+x^2} = x^2-1+\frac1{1+x^2}. \]
Hence \[ \int \frac{x^4}{1+x^2}\,dx = \int \left( x^2-1+\frac1{1+x^2} \right)dx. \]
Therefore \[ = \frac{x^3}{3}-x+\arctan x. \]
Substitute: \[ \int x^2\ln(1+x^2)\,dx = \frac{x^3}{3}\ln(1+x^2) - \frac23 \left( \frac{x^3}{3}-x+\arctan x \right)+C. \]
\[ \boxed{ \frac{x^3}{3}\ln(1+x^2) -\frac{2x^3}{9} +\frac{2x}{3} -\frac23\arctan x+C } \]
CHALLENGE 22
Evaluate \[ \int x^3e^{-x}\,dx. \]
FULL SOLUTION
Let \[ u=x^3,\qquad dv=e^{-x}\,dx. \]
Then \[ du=3x^2\,dx, \qquad v=-e^{-x}. \]
Therefore \[ \int x^3e^{-x}\,dx = -x^3e^{-x} + 3\int x^2e^{-x}\,dx. \]
Now \[ \int x^2e^{-x}\,dx = -x^2e^{-x} + 2\int xe^{-x}\,dx. \]
Also \[ \int xe^{-x}\,dx = -(x+1)e^{-x}. \]
Therefore \[ \int x^2e^{-x}\,dx = -x^2e^{-x} - 2(x+1)e^{-x}. \]
Hence \[ = -(x^2+2x+2)e^{-x}. \]
Substitute: \[ \int x^3e^{-x}\,dx = -x^3e^{-x} - 3(x^2+2x+2)e^{-x}+C. \]
Factor: \[ = -e^{-x} (x^3+3x^2+6x+6)+C. \]
\[ \boxed{ -e^{-x}(x^3+3x^2+6x+6)+C } \]
CHALLENGE 23
Evaluate \[ \int_0^1 x^2\ln x\,dx. \]
FULL SOLUTION
Choose \[ u=\ln x,\qquad dv=x^2\,dx. \]
Then \[ du=\frac1x\,dx, \qquad v=\frac{x^3}{3}. \]
Therefore \[ \int_0^1x^2\ln x\,dx = \left[ \frac{x^3}{3}\ln x - \frac13\int x^2\,dx \right]_0^1. \]
Integrate the remaining term: \[ \int x^2\,dx=\frac{x^3}{3}. \] Therefore \[ \int_0^1x^2\ln x\,dx = \left[ \frac{x^3}{3}\ln x - \frac{x^3}{9} \right]_0^1. \]
At \(x=1\): \[ \frac13\ln1-\frac19 = 0-\frac19 = -\frac19. \]
At \(x=0\), we consider the limiting value. Since \[ \lim_{x\to0^+}x^3\ln x=0, \] and \[ \lim_{x\to0^+}\frac{x^3}{9}=0, \] the lower limit is \[ 0. \]
Hence \[ -\frac19-0=-\frac19. \]
\[ \boxed{-\frac19} \]
CHALLENGE 24
Evaluate \[ \int e^x\cos x\,dx. \] Use integration by parts and show clearly what happens when the original integral appears again.
FULL SOLUTION
Let \[ I=\int e^x\cos x\,dx. \]
Choose \[ u=\cos x,\qquad dv=e^x\,dx. \]
Therefore \[ du=-\sin x\,dx, \qquad v=e^x. \]
Applying integration by parts: \[ I = e^x\cos x - \int e^x(-\sin x)\,dx. \]
Therefore \[ I = e^x\cos x + \int e^x\sin x\,dx. \]
Now evaluate \[ \int e^x\sin x\,dx. \] Let \[ J=\int e^x\sin x\,dx. \]
Using integration by parts again: \[ u=\sin x,\qquad dv=e^x\,dx. \] Hence \[ du=\cos x\,dx,\qquad v=e^x. \]
Thus \[ J=e^x\sin x-\int e^x\cos x\,dx. \]
Since \[ I=\int e^x\cos x\,dx, \] we have \[ J=e^x\sin x-I. \]
Substitute into the first equation: \[ I=e^x\cos x+e^x\sin x-I. \]
Add \(I\) to both sides: \[ 2I=e^x(\sin x+\cos x). \]
Divide by \(2\): \[ I= \frac12e^x(\sin x+\cos x)+C. \]
\[ \boxed{ \frac{e^x}{2}(\sin x+\cos x)+C } \]
