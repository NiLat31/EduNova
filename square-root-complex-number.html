Square Roots of Complex Numbers

A complex number can have square roots too. Learn how to find them, understand why they come in pairs, and check your answers with confidence.

01 · The big idea

What does a square root mean?

Finding a square root means answering this question: which number gives the original number when we multiply it by itself?

For example, the square roots of \(25\) are \(5\) and \(-5\), because both numbers produce \(25\) when squared.

\[5^2=25 \qquad (-5)^2=25\]

The same idea works with complex numbers. If we want the square roots of a complex number \(z\), we look for a complex number that gives \(z\) when squared.

Remember: Unless the number is zero, its two square roots are opposites. If one root is \(u+vi\), the other is \(-u-vi\).

One number

\[w=u+vi\]

This is the root we are trying to discover.

Square it

\[w^2=z\]

Our answer is correct when squaring it gives the original complex number.

02 · Build the method

Turn one complex equation into two real equations

Suppose we want to find the square roots of \(a+bi\), where \(a\) and \(b\) are real numbers. We do not know the answer yet, so give its square root a name:

\[\sqrt{a+bi}=x+yi\]

Here, \(x\) is the real part and \(y\) is the coefficient of the imaginary part. Now square both sides.

\[(x+yi)^2=a+bi\]

Expand the left side carefully. Remember that \(i^2=-1\).

\[\begin{aligned}(x+yi)^2&=x^2+2xyi+y^2i^2\\&=x^2-y^2+2xyi\end{aligned}\]

Now compare the real parts with each other, and the imaginary parts with each other.

Real parts

\[\boxed{x^2-y^2=a}\]

Imaginary parts

\[\boxed{2xy=b}\]
Why can we compare them? Two complex numbers are equal only when their real parts match and their imaginary coefficients match. For example, \(3+4i=3+4i\), but \(3+4i\ne3+5i\).

Our solving plan

Write the unknown root
Let it be \(x+yi\), with \(x,y\in\mathbb R\).

Square and expand
Use \(i^2=-1\) to simplify the expression.

Compare the two parts
Obtain \(x^2-y^2=a\) and \(2xy=b\).

Solve for \(x\) and \(y\)
Use the equations together, then check the result by squaring.

Give both roots
If one root is \(x+yi\), the other is \(-x-yi\).

Quick check: which pair of equations is correct?

After expanding \((x+yi)^2=a+bi\), choose the correct comparison.

03 · Follow a complete solution

Worked example

Find the two square roots of:

\[7+24i\]

We will build the answer from the two equations rather than guess it.

Solution, step by stepExample 01
Step 1 · Set up the unknown
Let one square root be \(x+yi\).

Then \((x+yi)^2=7+24i\).

Step 2 · Expand
\[x^2-y^2+2xyi=7+24i\]

Match the real and imaginary parts:

\[x^2-y^2=7 \qquad 2xy=24\]
Step 3 · Simplify the second equation
\[2xy=24 \quad\Rightarrow\quad xy=12\]

So \(y=\dfrac{12}{x}\). We can substitute this into the first equation.

Step 4 · Substitute and solve
\[x^2-\left(\frac{12}{x}\right)^2=7\]
\[x^2-\frac{144}{x^2}=7\]

Multiply every term by \(x^2\):

\[x^4-144=7x^2\]
\[x^4-7x^2-144=0\]

Factorise:

\[(x^2-16)(x^2+9)=0\]

Therefore \(x^2=16\) or \(x^2=-9\). Since \(x\) is real, \(x^2=-9\) is impossible. Thus \(x=4\) or \(x=-4\).

Step 5 · Find the matching value of \(y\)

Use \(xy=12\).

If \(x=4\), then \(y=3\).
If \(x=-4\), then \(y=-3\).

The two roots are therefore opposites.

Step 6 · State and verify
\[\boxed{4+3i\quad\text{and}\quad -4-3i}\]

Check the first root:

\[(4+3i)^2=16+24i+9i^2=7+24i\]

It works. The negative root also works because squaring a negative gives the same result.

Key lesson: We did not choose \(x=4\) randomly. The equations led us to \(x^2=16\), and the second equation told us which value of \(y\) belongs with each value of \(x\).
04 · A useful shortcut

A faster route when the numbers are manageable

There is another way to organise the same mathematics. It can save time once you understand where it comes from.

For \(z=a+bi\), first calculate its modulus:

\[r=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}\]

If \(b\ne0\), the square root with positive real part is:

\[\boxed{x=\sqrt{\frac{r+a}{2}},\qquad y=\operatorname{sgn}(b)\sqrt{\frac{r-a}{2}}}\]

So the two roots are \(x+yi\) and \(-x-yi\). The sign of \(b\) determines the sign of \(y\) in the root with positive real part.

Do not memorise this before understanding the method. The shortcut is a compact form of the same two equations. It is especially useful for checking answers and handling larger coefficients.

Try the shortcut with \(7+24i\)

\[r=\sqrt{7^2+24^2}=\sqrt{625}=25\]
\[x=\sqrt{\frac{25+7}{2}}=4,\qquad y=\sqrt{\frac{25-7}{2}}=3\]

Therefore the roots are \(4+3i\) and \(-4-3i\), exactly as before.

05 · Your turn

Practice makes the method yours

For Questions 1–6, enter the values of \(x\) and \(y\) for the root written as \(x+yi\) with positive real part. The other root is its negative.

Level 1 · Build confidenceFoundation

1. Find the square roots of \(3-4i\). Enter \(x\) and \(y\) for \(x+yi\).

Let the root be \(x+yi\). Comparing parts gives \(x^2-y^2=3\) and \(2xy=-4\), so \(xy=-2\). The values \(x=2,y=-1\) satisfy both: \(4-1=3\) and \(2(2)(-1)=-4\).

\[\boxed{2-i\quad\text{and}\quad -2+i}\]

2. Find the square roots of \(7-24i\).

The equations are \(x^2-y^2=7\) and \(2xy=-24\). The pair \(x=4,y=-3\) gives \(16-9=7\) and \(2(4)(-3)=-24\).

\[\boxed{4-3i\quad\text{and}\quad -4+3i}\]
Level 2 · Stretch your skillsIntermediate

3. Find the square roots of \(85+132i\).

We need \(x^2-y^2=85\) and \(2xy=132\), so \(xy=66\). The pair \(11,6\) works: \(121-36=85\) and \(2(11)(6)=132\).

\[\boxed{11+6i\quad\text{and}\quad -11-6i}\]

4. Find the square roots of \(336+320i\).

The equations are \(x^2-y^2=336\) and \(2xy=320\), giving \(xy=160\). The pair \(20,8\) works because \(400-64=336\) and \(2(20)(8)=320\).

\[\boxed{20+8i\quad\text{and}\quad -20-8i}\]
Level 3 · Work backwardsAdvanced

5. The two square roots of \(55-48i\) are \(a-3i\) and \(b+ci\). Find \(a\), \(b\) and \(c\).

Square \(a-3i\): \(a^2-9-6ai=55-48i\). Comparing real parts gives \(a^2=64\); comparing imaginary coefficients gives \(-6a=-48\), so \(a=8\). The other square root is the negative: \(-8+3i\). Hence \(b=-8\) and \(c=3\).

\[\boxed{a=8,\quad b=-8,\quad c=3}\]
Level 4 · Think like a mathematicianReasoning

6. A student expands \((x+yi)^2\) and writes \(x^2+y^2+2xyi\). What is the mistake?

The expansion is \(x^2+2xyi+y^2i^2\). Since \(i^2=-1\), it becomes \(x^2-y^2+2xyi\). The student's error is treating \(i^2\) as \(+1\).

7. If \(w\) is a square root of a non-zero complex number \(z\), what is the other square root?

If \(w^2=z\), then \((-w)^2=(-1)^2w^2=w^2=z\). Thus the two roots are \(w\) and \(-w\). A conjugate is not generally the other root.

Self-check tip: Once you have a proposed root \(x+yi\), square it. If the result is the original complex number, your root is correct. Then include its negative as the second root.
